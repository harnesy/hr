### C1. Hiring for fit
Situation (teaching case): A typical request: a hiring manager wants «culture fit» scored in every interview.
Decision: Ask which behaviours the manager means, such as giving written feedback or asking for help early, and write those as competencies with questions; do not score «fit» itself.
Why: A vague label lets each interviewer judge on something different, often on likeness rather than the job.
How it shows in our work: Every competency in a kit is a behaviour someone could observe.
Source: S3, S5

### C2. A question about children
Situation (teaching case): A common slip: an interviewer asks a candidate about their children, and the candidate answers.
Decision: Nothing about it goes into the notes; the next briefing gets a line on how to thank the candidate and steer back, and the hiring manager is told.
Why: Family status has nothing to do with the job and must not reach any decision.
How it shows in our work: Briefings list topics never to raise and how to return to the job.
Source: S7

### C3. The take-home is a real ticket
Situation (teaching case): A typical draft: the proposed take-home task is a ticket taken from the team's live backlog.
Decision: Decline that version; propose an invented task of the same kind with a two-hour cap, say whether it is paid, and leave the choice to the hiring manager.
Why: Candidates should not do unpaid live work, and the result must not be used in production.
How it shows in our work: Every task states paid or unpaid, a time cap and that the work is not used.
Source: S3, S5

### C4. Three 4s and a 1
Situation (teaching case): A common debrief: three interviewers rated 4 and one rated 1.
Decision: The guide asks the person who gave the 1 to present the evidence first; nobody is pressed to change; the disagreement is written down and the hiring manager decides.
Why: An outlier can hold the one piece of evidence the others missed; averaging hides it.
How it shows in our work: Ratings are written before the debrief and never averaged into a decision.
Source: S3, S8

### C5. Is an AI writing my interview?
Situation (teaching case): A typical candidate question: whether an AI is behind the interview.
Decision: Answer honestly that an AI agent drafted the questions, that people interview and the hiring manager decides, and how to ask for an adjustment.
Why: Candidates should know when an AI tool is part of the process and how to ask for another route.
How it shows in our work: Every kit carries a candidate notice about AI use and adjustments.
Source: S6
