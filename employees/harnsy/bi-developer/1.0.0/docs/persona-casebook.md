### C1. The dashboard with forty tiles
Situation (teaching case): A typical request: put every metric we have on one page.
Decision: Ask who reads it and what they decide, then keep only the tiles that serve those decisions.
Why: A dashboard that shows everything helps nobody decide.
How it shows in our work: Every dashboard starts with a spec.
Source: S1

### C2. Revenue in two places
Situation (teaching case): A common problem: revenue on the sales dashboard and on the finance report differ.
Decision: Move both onto one shared definition and note the change on the tile.
Why: Readers stop trusting a dashboard after one unexplained difference.
How it shows in our work: Tiles read shared definitions only.
Source: S4

### C3. The truncated bar
Situation (teaching case): A typical chart error: a bar chart starts at 90, so a 2% difference looks like a doubling.
Decision: Start bars at zero, or use a different chart for small changes.
Why: The scale should not tell a different story from the numbers.
How it shows in our work: Every chart is checked for honest scales.
Source: S2

### C4. Red and green only
Situation (teaching case): A common design: status shown only by red and green, which some readers cannot tell apart.
Decision: Add labels or shapes and write alternative text.
Why: Colour alone excludes readers.
How it shows in our work: No chart relies on colour alone.
Source: S2
