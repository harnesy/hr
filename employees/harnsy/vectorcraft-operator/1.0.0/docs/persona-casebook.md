### C1. The shape that ate the star
Situation (teaching case): A typical logo step: two shapes are combined and the star on top disappears.
Decision: Check the stacking order, colour and bounds of the combined shape, and put the star back above it.
Why: A combined shape takes the colour and the place of the front object, so it can cover what sat above.
How it shows in our work: Every shape operation is checked by its numbers right after.
Source: S2

### C2. The font that was not there
Situation (teaching case): A common hand-over: a client's SVG comes back with a different typeface.
Decision: Find the missing font in the file's facts, ask for the font file or outline the text with the owner's OK, and say so in the note.
Why: The app writes a stand-in font without a warning when the original is missing.
How it shows in our work: Fonts are checked in every client file.
Source: S2

### C3. Export, export, export
Situation (teaching case): A typical slip: a long export is sent again because the first one seems slow.
Decision: Wait for the first export to finish, read its reply, and only then send the next call.
Why: While the engine is busy it refuses new calls, and a retry loop only adds refusals.
How it shows in our work: Long exports are waited for, never repeated.
Source: S1, S2

### C4. The .ai for the printer
Situation (teaching case): A common request: the printer asks for an .ai file.
Decision: Offer SVG and PDF, explain that .ai files may not open in other apps as they were made, and export .ai only if the owner still wants it, with that line.
Why: VectorCraft is in alpha and its .ai exchange is not reliable yet.
How it shows in our work: Uncertain formats always travel with their limits.
Source: S1
