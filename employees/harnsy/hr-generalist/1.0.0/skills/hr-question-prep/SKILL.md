# HR question prep

People questions often feel urgent and often depend on the law of one country. This skill turns a question into a short, confidential brief: what happened, what the dated sources say, the options, and what a professional must answer. The decision stays with the owner and that professional.

**Route:** question → (country first) → **hr-question-prep** → the owner (and a professional) decide → the agent drafts what the decision needs.
Before this skill: a question from a manager, an employee or the owner. After it: the owner's decision, then drafts (a letter, a checklist, a policy change) for the person to send or sign.

## When to use

- A manager asks whether they can do something about a person (change hours, refuse leave, give a warning, end a contract).
- An employee asks about their rights, pay, leave or a concern.
- A situation needs a decision and nobody has written down the facts.

## Rules

- **Country first.** Ask for the country (and region), company size and the kind of contract before saying anything about a rule.
- **Confidential.** Only the people who need it see the brief. Only the data the question needs; no health or other sensitive details unless the question is about them and the owner's rule allows it. Nothing leaves the project.
- **The person decides.** Hiring, dismissal, discipline, pay, leave and contract decisions are the owner's, with a professional where the law matters. The brief offers options; it does not choose.
- **No final legal answer.** Never say an action is allowed, safe, or meets the law. Say what the dated sources say and what must be confirmed.
- **Fair process.** The person concerned gets a chance to give their side before any decision about them, as the owner's process says.

## Steps

1. **Ask the starting questions:** country, size, contract type, who is asking, and what decision is needed by when.
2. **Write the facts:** what happened, when, who was involved, what is written down (contract, policy, messages). Keep facts apart from opinions, and mark what is not known.
3. **Find the rules that apply:** the company's own policies and the contract first, then official sources for that country (government or regulator pages), each with its date and any «draft» or «under review» banner. A source that cannot be fetched is named, not quoted.
4. **Write the options:** two or three ways forward, each with what it involves, its effect on the person and the company, and what must be checked first. Include «do nothing yet» when it is real.
5. **Write the questions for a professional:** each one short, with the facts and sources it depends on.
6. **Send the brief** to the owner. Record the decision when it comes; then draft what it needs for the person to send or sign.

## Checks before hand-over

- Country, contract type and the decision needed are stated.
- Facts and opinions are separated; unknowns are marked.
- Every rule cited has a source and a date.
- No statement that an action is allowed, safe or meets the law.
- The brief holds only the data the question needs, and is stored only where the owner's rule says.

## Artefacts

- `hr-brief-<date>.md`: facts, sources, options, questions for a professional, the decision needed and by when.
- After the decision: drafts (letter, checklist, policy change) marked as drafts for the person.

## Hand-over

- **To the owner:** the brief. The owner decides and, where the law matters, consults a professional.
- **To `policy-draft`:** when the question shows a policy is missing or unclear.
- **To the recruiter or the onboarding specialist:** when the decision changes a hiring or joining step.
