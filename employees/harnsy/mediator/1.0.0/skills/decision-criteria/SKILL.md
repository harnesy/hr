# Decision criteria

Once each side has been understood, the question changes from «who is right» to «which option does best on what we both care about». This skill builds that comparison: criteria both sides accept, the weight each side gives them, and scores with evidence, so the decider can see exactly where the sides agree and where they differ.

**Route:** `position-summary` → **decision-criteria** → `decision-note`.

## When to use

- After both position summaries are confirmed.
- When a decider asks for the options to be compared on agreed terms.
- Not to choose an option: the decider chooses.

## Inputs

- The confirmed summaries and shared facts.
- The options on the table, at least two; «keep things as they are» always counts as one.
- The decider's name, or the question of who decides.

## Steps

1. **Settle the level:** does the decision belong to one team, several teams or the organisation? That tells you who decides and who must be consulted.
2. **Collect criteria:** ask both sides what any good outcome must achieve (for example cost, time, risk, effect on users, who has to do the work, how easy it is to reverse). Keep only those both accept; list the rest as «one side only».
3. **Collect weights from each side** separately. Show both sets; do not average them.
4. **Score each option** against each criterion, with the evidence for each score (an estimate, a measurement, a document). Mark scores that are estimates.
5. **Offer a private round** when one side may not speak freely: scores are given separately and opened together.
6. **Check the familiar option:** if the scores lean towards the current way of doing things, ask whether that is evidence or habit, and say what you found.
7. **Name the decider and the date** the decision is needed.
8. **Recommend nothing** unless the decider asks; if asked, label it clearly as a recommendation with its reasons.

## Rules

- **Both accept every criterion.** A criterion only one side accepts is shown, but not scored as shared.
- **Differences stay visible.** Different weights are shown side by side.
- **Evidence for every score,** or the score is marked as an estimate.
- **The mediator does not choose,** and does not steer the scores.
- **Familiar is not the same as better.** The status-quo check is written into the table.

## Checks before hand-over

- Every scored criterion is accepted by both sides.
- Both sides' weights are shown.
- Every score has evidence or an «estimate» mark.
- The decider and the date are named.
- The table contains no choice by the mediator.

## Artefacts

- `mediation/<topic>/criteria.md`: decision level, criteria (shared and one-sided), weights per side, option scores with evidence, the familiar-option check, decider and date.

## Hand-over

- **To `decision-note`:** the table.
- **To the decider:** the table, if the decider wants it before the note.
