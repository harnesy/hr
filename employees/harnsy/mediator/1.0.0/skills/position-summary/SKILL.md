# Position summary

Most disagreements stay stuck because each side thinks the other has not understood. This skill fixes that first: each side's case is written so well that its holder says «yes, that is it», and only then does anyone compare. It works the same for two people and for two agents whose outputs conflict.

**Route:** a disagreement → **position-summary** → `decision-criteria` → `decision-note`.

## When to use

- At the start of mediating a disagreement about work: approach, priorities, ownership, conflicting outputs.
- When a decider asks for both sides to be set out fairly before deciding.
- Not for disagreements about pay, dismissal, conduct, harassment or legal claims, or where someone may be vulnerable: those go to a person at once.

## Inputs

- The disagreement as raised, and who raised it.
- The sides (people or agents) and their messages, documents or outputs.
- Who will decide, if known.

## Steps

1. **Say what will be shared:** before anyone speaks, tell each side what will be passed on and to whom (normally: the confirmed summary, to the other side and the decider). Keep to it.
2. **Hear each side separately,** or read its material separately.
3. **Write each position in its strongest form,** from that side's own words: what it wants, why, the evidence it relies on, what it fears if the other option wins, what it could live with.
4. **Mark facts, interpretations and wishes** apart: a fact can be checked, an interpretation is a reading of facts, a wish is what someone wants.
5. **Send each summary only to its own side** and correct it until that side confirms it in writing. Add nothing the side did not say.
6. **List the facts both sides accept.**
7. **Share both confirmed summaries** with both sides.
8. **List the open questions** that block a decision, with who could answer each.

## Rules

- **Strongest form.** No summary is weaker than its holder's own wording.
- **Confirmed before shared.** A summary goes to the other side only after its holder confirms it.
- **No new arguments.** The summary carries the side's case, not the mediator's improvements to it.
- **Private stays private.** Remarks outside the agreed sharing stay out of the record unless the speaker agrees.
- **No promise that cannot be kept.** If something must be passed on (for example a safety issue), say so before anyone speaks.
- **Out of scope stops the skill.** Pay, dismissal, conduct, harassment, legal claims or a vulnerable person: stop and hand to a person.

## Checks before hand-over

- Each summary is confirmed by its own side in writing.
- Facts, interpretations and wishes are marked.
- Shared facts and open questions are listed.
- Nothing was shared beyond what was announced.

## Artefacts

- `mediation/<topic>/positions.md`: what is shared and with whom, two confirmed summaries, shared facts, open questions.

## Hand-over

- **To `decision-criteria`:** the confirmed summaries and shared facts.
- **To both sides:** both confirmed summaries.
- **To the HR generalist:** any matter about pay, dismissal, conduct, harassment or legal claims, with only what the speaker agreed to share.
