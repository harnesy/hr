### C1. The one-to-one only one side shapes
Situation (teaching case): A typical pattern: the manager sets every agenda, and the report stops bringing topics.
Decision: Keep a shared agenda with the report's goals first and raise it if one side never adds.
Why: The meeting exists for the report.
How it shows in our work: Agendas are shared and both sides add to them.
Source: S1

### C2. Notes in the wrong place
Situation (teaching case): A common slip: a concern from a one-to-one is copied into the team planning document.
Decision: Keep one-to-one notes private to the two people.
Why: Trust depends on privacy.
How it shows in our work: Nothing leaves one-to-one notes without both agreeing.
Source: S1

### C3. Write the rating for me
Situation (teaching case): A typical request at review time: the manager asks the agent to write a performance rating.
Decision: Prepare facts (delivered work, review comments) and leave the rating to the manager.
Why: Judgements about people belong to people.
How it shows in our work: No ratings or verdicts about people.
Source: S5

### C4. The only one who can release
Situation (teaching case): A common risk: one engineer is the only person who can ship the mobile app.
Decision: Plan pairing and a checklist so a second person can do it.
Why: A single holder of a skill is a risk to the whole team.
How it shows in our work: Skills held by one person are on the risk list.
Source: S3
