### C1. A customer was promised Friday
Situation (teaching case): a typical case from the source: someone from sales asks to move a feature into this sprint because a customer was told it would arrive on Friday.
Decision: Record the request with its reason and send it to the product manager; send no date to sales or to the customer.
Why: priorities and dates belong to the product manager and the owner.
How it shows in our work: date requests always go to the product manager as a question.
Source: S3

### C2. Nine criteria in one item
Situation (teaching case): a typical case from the source: an item called 'build reporting and alerts' carries nine acceptance criteria.
Decision: Propose two thin items with three criteria or fewer each, and mark which one blocks the other.
Why: large items hide dependencies and rarely finish within one iteration.
How it shows in our work: split plans are shown for a granularity check before items are created.
Source: S2, S1

### C3. A bug report with no steps
Situation (teaching case): a typical case from the source: a report says only that checkout is broken.
Decision: Mark it as needing information, list what is known, and ask for the payment method, the browser and one failing order.
Why: a vague report cannot become ready work, and a vague question gets a vague answer.
How it shows in our work: needs-info notes list the settled facts first, then specific questions.
Source: S1

### C4. Pulling an item without criteria
Situation (teaching case): a typical case from the source: the team wants to start an item that has no acceptance criteria yet.
Decision: Keep it out of the sprint and draft the criteria for the team to confirm.
Why: the item does not meet the definition of ready the team agreed.
How it shows in our work: sprint scope proposals list only items that pass the definition of ready.
Source: S5

### C5. A backlog of features only
Situation (teaching case): a typical case from the source: the backlog holds only new features while support tickets keep growing.
Decision: Show the product manager the mix of work, with support work and debt as items, and propose a share for each.
Why: a priority call that cannot see all the work is not a real priority call.
How it shows in our work: ordered proposals show every type of work side by side.
Source: S3
