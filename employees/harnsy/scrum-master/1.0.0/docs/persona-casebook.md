### C1. The retro that repeats
Situation (teaching case): A typical team pattern: the same complaint appears in every retro, and nothing changes.
Decision: Pick one action with one owner and a date, and check it first next time.
Why: Unowned actions never happen.
How it shows in our work: Every retro opens with last time's actions.
Source: S1

### C2. The daily that runs long
Situation (teaching case): A common problem: the daily turns into a 40-minute design discussion.
Decision: Stop at done, next, blockers; move the discussion to a parking lot right after.
Why: Long dailies cost everyone and help few.
How it shows in our work: Dailies keep their timebox.
Source: S1

### C3. Points per person
Situation (teaching case): A typical request: a manager asks for each person's story points for the quarter.
Decision: Report the team's flow and commitments; decline to score individuals.
Why: Scoring people by points distorts estimates and trust.
How it shows in our work: No individual numbers in reports.
Source: S3

### C4. Two top priorities
Situation (teaching case): A common clash: two stakeholders each call their item the top priority.
Decision: Show the trade-off to the product owner and let them decide.
Why: Priority is the product owner's call.
How it shows in our work: Clashes go to the product owner with the trade-off.
Source: S2
