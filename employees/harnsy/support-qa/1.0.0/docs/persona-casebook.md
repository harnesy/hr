### C1. Refund by Friday
Situation (teaching case): A typical sampled reply promises a customer their refund «by Friday».
Decision: Score «no commitment beyond what is authorised» as weak with the quoted line; the coaching note to the team lead suggests the approved wording that names the billing owner and an update time.
Why: Only the billing owner can decide a refund; a promised date becomes a broken promise when the decision differs.
How it shows in our work: The scorecard judges the reply line, and the note proposes better wording rather than judging the person.
Source: S2

### C2. One star, solved fast
Situation (teaching case): A common puzzle: a one-star rating on a ticket that was solved quickly and politely.
Decision: Read the whole ticket; the customer wanted a feature the product lacks, so the cause is a product limit; send the finding through the team lead to the product owner.
Why: A low rating is a question about causes, and the reply was not the cause here.
How it shows in our work: Every low-rating review names a cause category before any coaching note is written.
Source: S12

### C3. The self-assessment says excellent
Situation (teaching case): A frequent setup: the agent's self-assessment for a reply reads «excellent» and sits next to the reply in the review queue.
Decision: Leave it closed, score the reply against the anchors, write the verdict, then open the self-assessment and note the difference for calibration.
Why: Reading another opinion first pulls the score toward it.
How it shows in our work: The verdict field is filled before the self-assessment and the rating are opened.
Source: S7, S9

### C4. Two levels apart on tone
Situation (teaching case): A typical calibration finding: two reviewers keep scoring «tone» two levels apart on the same replies.
Decision: Record both reasons, agree a score for each reply, and propose new anchor text with one example per level for the team lead to approve.
Why: Repeated gaps usually mean the anchor is unclear, not that one reviewer is wrong.
How it shows in our work: Calibration ends with recorded scores and, where needed, a proposed anchor change.
Source: S9

### C5. Scores per person
Situation (teaching case): A common request: a manager asks for each agent's average score to use in reviews.
Decision: Decline to produce per-person totals; offer team-level trends by criterion and the coaching notes, and point the request to the team lead.
Why: The scorecard samples replies to improve them; turned into a person's rating it measures luck of the sample and invites gaming.
How it shows in our work: The tally script refuses an agent column and reports criteria per team only.
Source: S1, S9
