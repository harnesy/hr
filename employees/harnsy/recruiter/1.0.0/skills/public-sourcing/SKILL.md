# Public sourcing

Sourcing widens the pool; it does not decide anything about anyone. It works only from what people have chosen to make public about their work, and it never contacts anyone by itself.

**Route:** job-post (approved criteria) → **public-sourcing** → the person sends outreach → applications → screening-notes.
Before this skill: approved criteria from `job-post` and the person's request to source. After it: the person picks whom to contact and sends the messages.

## When to use

- The hiring manager asks for more candidates than the applications bring, or for people the usual channels do not reach.
- Only after the criteria are approved: sourcing against unwritten criteria is guesswork.

## Rules

- **Public professional information only:** profiles people published for work, open-source contributions, talks, articles, public portfolios, professional directories.
- **Never:** private social profiles, anything behind a login you would have to work around, scraped or bought contact lists, guessed e-mail addresses, information about family, health, religion, politics or other private life.
- **No contact without the person's go.** You write drafts; the person decides whom to contact and sends the message.
- **No protected traits.** Do not record or guess age, sex, origin, health, family status or similar, and do not search by them. Do not filter by photo or name.
- **Wide, not narrow.** Search in more than one kind of place, so the list does not come from one network of people who already know each other.
- **Data kept short.** Only what the step needs, inside the project, deleted when the owner's rule says.

## Steps

1. **Turn criteria into searches.** For each must-have, list where public evidence of it would show (code, writing, talks, certifications the work needs) and write the queries.
2. **Search and record.** For each person: name as they publish it, the public link, the date you saw it, and which criteria the public evidence speaks to, with the evidence quoted or linked. Write «unclear» where public information says nothing.
3. **Check the sheet.** Remove anything private, anything about a protected trait, and anyone who asked not to be contacted by recruiters. Do not rank the list.
4. **Write outreach drafts.** Short and specific: why this person's public work is relevant, what the role is, the pay band, a line that an AI agent helped prepare the message (the owner chooses the wording), and an easy way to say no. One draft per person, no templates sent in bulk.
5. **Hand over and wait.** Send the sheet and drafts to the hiring manager. Record who the person chose to contact and when it was sent.

## Checks before hand-over

- Every row has a public link and a date.
- No private information, no protected trait, no guessed contact details.
- No ranking and no score.
- Every outreach draft names the role, the pay band and how to decline.
- Every draft says an AI agent helped prepare it.
- Nobody has been contacted.

## Artefacts

- `sourcing-sheet.md` (or a table): person, public link, date seen, criteria with public evidence, «unclear» where nothing is public.
- `outreach/`: one draft per person.

## Hand-over

- **To the hiring manager:** the sheet and drafts. The person decides whom to contact and sends the messages.
- **To `screening-notes`:** people who reply and apply, through the normal application route, so they are judged on the same criteria as everyone else.
