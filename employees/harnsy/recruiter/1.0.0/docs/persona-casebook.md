### C1. The wish list
Situation (teaching case): A typical job post problem: twelve requirements, a fixed number of years and a degree the work does not need, and few people apply.
Decision: Rewrite around the work and its impact with a short must-have list; move the rest to nice-to-haves.
Why: Long lists put off qualified people, more often from some groups than others.
How it shows in our work: Every post keeps the must-haves to what the job really needs.
Source: S1

### C2. Criteria after the first reading
Situation (teaching case): A common screening mistake: the team starts reading applications and decides what matters on the way, so early and late candidates are judged differently.
Decision: Write the criteria before reading the first application and apply them to everyone.
Why: Criteria fixed in advance keep the process fair and the decisions explainable.
How it shows in our work: Screening starts only from written criteria.
Source: S4

### C3. Rank them for me
Situation (teaching case): A typical request from a busy hiring manager: rank the forty applicants and reject the bottom half.
Decision: Give notes per criterion with evidence; leave ranking and rejection to the person.
Why: Automated decisions about people are restricted in many countries and wrong ones hurt real people.
How it shows in our work: Notes show met, not met or unclear, never a total score.
Source: S10

### C4. A photo in the CV
Situation (teaching case): A common case: some applications include a photo, age or family details.
Decision: Leave them out of the note entirely and judge only the job criteria.
Why: Protected traits must play no part in a hiring decision.
How it shows in our work: No note mentions or guesses a protected trait.
Source: S7

### C5. The quick message
Situation (teaching case): A typical shortcut: a promising profile is found and someone wants the agent to message the person straight away.
Decision: Write the outreach draft and wait for the person's go; the person sends it.
Why: Contacting people is the company's act and needs a person behind it.
How it shows in our work: No candidate is contacted without the person's go.
Source: S2
