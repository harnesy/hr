# Onboarding plan

A newcomer's first weeks go well when the people around them know who does what, and when. The plan puts that on one page. It is a working plan, not an assessment: decisions about the person (probation, ratings, pay, contract) are made by the manager and HR, never in this plan.

**Route:** accepted offer (from the hiring manager, HR generalist or recruiter) → **onboarding-plan** → the manager approves → `first-week-checklist` → `check-in-notes` at about 30, 60 and 90 days → hand-over to the manager's ongoing one-to-ones.

## When to use

- An offer is accepted and the start date is known.
- Someone moves into a new team or role and needs a fresh start plan.
- Not for policy or contract questions: those go to the HR generalist (`hr-question-prep`, `handbook-section`).

## Inputs

Collect these first; anything missing is a gap with an owner, not a guess:

- name (or the reference the team uses), role, team, start date;
- manager, and a buddy if one is already agreed;
- location, working hours and time zone; remote, hybrid or on site;
- tools and access the role needs, and who owns each one;
- the team's onboarding guide, if there is one;
- any adjustment the newcomer has asked for, passed on as written.

## Steps

1. **Draft expectations for the manager to set.** What the role is responsible for, and what good looks like at about 30, 60 and 90 days. Mark each line «set by <manager>». The manager edits and approves; if expectations change later, the newcomer is told.
2. **Before day one.** Access requested from each owner, equipment ordered, recurring meetings in the calendar (a one-to-one with the manager, team meetings), a buddy asked and confirmed, a short welcome note with first-day logistics.
3. **Day one.** Keep it light: setup, meeting the team, and a conversation with the manager about expectations. Deep work waits. For a remote newcomer, plan the day in their hours.
4. **Week one.** Reading for the role (short and relevant), meetings to sit in on, introductions to the people the newcomer will work with, one small real task with a clear finish, and an end-of-week check-in. Where the role allows, early contact with real users or customers, with someone alongside.
5. **The buddy.** A colleague who is not the newcomer's manager, who has agreed and has time, with work questions and social time planned in the first week. Plan overlap hours across time zones.
6. **Check-ins.** About 30, 60 and 90 days, two-way, with `check-in-notes`. Regular one-to-ones run in between on a steady rhythm.
7. **Owners and dates.** Every item names who does it (manager, buddy, IT or the tool owner, HR, the newcomer) and by when.
8. **After day 90.** Ask the newcomer what to change for the next person, and update the team's template.

## Rules

- The plan holds work items only. It never mentions or guesses health, family or any protected trait.
- Access and accounts are requested from their owners; the agent never grants or changes access itself.
- Nothing in the plan is a rating or a probation verdict.

## Checks before hand-over

- Every item has an owner and a date.
- Expectations are marked «set by <manager>» and approved before day one.
- A buddy has said yes, or the gap is flagged to the manager.
- Day one respects the newcomer's time zone and any adjustment asked for.
- Week one has one real task with a clear finish.

## Artefacts

- `onboarding/<newcomer-ref>/plan.md`: facts, expectations, phases, owners and dates.
- `onboarding/<newcomer-ref>/gaps.md`: missing facts, each with an owner.

## Hand-over

- **To the manager:** the plan, to approve and to set the expectations.
- **To the HR generalist:** policy and contract items raised while planning.
- **To `first-week-checklist`:** the approved plan, about a week before the start.
- **To the learning designer:** a request for a course, when the role needs real training rather than a reading list.
