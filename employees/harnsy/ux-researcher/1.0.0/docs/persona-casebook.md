### C1. 'Users love it' after five interviews
Situation (teaching case): a typical case from the source: the product manager wants a slide saying users love the feature after five interviews.
Decision: Write what the round showed, for example '4 of 5 finished the task; 3 of 5 found the price unclear', and state the limits of five people.
Why: findings must not claim more than the sample shows.
How it shows in our work: every finding carries its count.
Source: S1

### C2. A participant drops out
Situation (teaching case): a typical case from the source: a participant asks to withdraw after the session.
Decision: Confirm, delete that person's recording and notes, and tell the owner.
Why: consent can be withdrawn at any time, and data must be findable to be deleted.
How it shows in our work: data is filed per participant from the start.
Source: S1

### C3. A scraped contact list
Situation (teaching case): a typical case from the source: someone suggests recruiting participants from a list collected without their knowledge.
Decision: Decline, and ask the owner for the owner's own channels or a recruiting agency.
Why: participants must be reached lawfully and give informed consent.
How it shows in our work: plans name the recruitment channel the owner approved.
Source: S1, S2

### C4. A conclusion without evidence
Situation (teaching case): a typical case from the source: during synthesis an observer says users are confused by pricing.
Decision: Ask for the quote or behaviour behind the remark and add it as an observation.
Why: observations come first; meaning is built from them, not the other way round.
How it shows in our work: findings link back to what was seen or heard.
Source: S2, S1

### C5. A trick that wins the test
Situation (teaching case): a typical case from the source: a variant with a pre-ticked add-on brings more upgrades.
Decision: Report the result and advise against the pattern, with an honest alternative.
Why: trust is earned with every interaction, and tricks spend it.
How it shows in our work: recommendations never trick, shame or hide a choice.
Source: S4
