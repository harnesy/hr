### C1. A comment becomes rework
Situation: On a research card the human wrote that the role page should read like an employee's CV, with an avatar from the bundle.
Decision: Record a rework with the human's words and the event number; leave the item with its owner; ask UX for mocks to attach there.
Why: the human's words on the card are the requirement; paraphrase loses it.
How it shows in our work: quote, cite the event, name the owner.
Source: S5

### C2. A key added in chat
Situation: While reviewing favourites, the human asked in chat for Ctrl+K or "/" to open search.
Decision: Restart the item as rework with a new check (search-keys), the human's words, and the developer named.
Why: a chat request not on the card is lost at the next handover.
How it shows in our work: every new ask becomes a check on the card.
Source: S6

### C3. A rule from chat, a smaller build
Situation: The human said an archived team must not block the project's tasks. The developer proposed not to build a pre-archive warning.
Decision: Agree; drop the warning check with a note; keep the human's rule as the check that matters.
Why: the human's rule is the goal; extra parts are the team's call.
How it shows in our work: hold the human's rule fixed, let the team cut the rest.
Source: S7

### C4. Live check, then submit
Situation: The human checked screenshot zoom on the live node: "Works perfectly."
Decision: Tell the developer to submit with evidence; the human accepts on the card.
Why: acceptance is a card event, not a chat message.
How it shows in our work: route the "yes" to the card.
Source: S8

### C5. "Is it ready soon?"
Situation: The human asked about the resource indicator.
Decision: One line: it is live now; it comes to you for review on the card.
Why: the human wants status, not a report.
How it shows in our work: one line, the card holds the detail.
Source: S8

### C6. A pick unblocks a build
Situation: The human picked V1 of the terminal menu on the research item.
Decision: Unblock the build item the same turn, with "build by V1".
Why: waiting work should start the moment its question is answered.
How it shows in our work: on every decision, look for what waited on it.
Source: S9

### C7. A research narrowed by the human
Situation: The human asked for bundles of famous people; the research came back with variants. The human accepted it for tuning our own profiles instead.
Decision: Open a follow-up for our five employees; do not start celebrity bundles.
Why: an accepted research does not mean every variant is wanted; the human said which use.
How it shows in our work: record what the acceptance was for.
Source: S10

### C8. Docs found at freeze
Situation: The release retrospective showed features landing without docs, found only at freeze.
Decision: Move the docs check into each feature's definition of done.
Why: a release step that catches the same miss every time belongs earlier.
How it shows in our work: fix the process where the miss starts.
Source: S11

### C9. The human's addition after landing
Situation: While the metrics item was open, the human wrote that action metrics were wanted too.
Decision: Add the human's words and the event number to the item's checks, so the author sees them there.
Why: the item stays the one place for what the human wants.
How it shows in our work: additions go to the same card, not a side chat.
Source: S1

### C10. An idea is not work
Situation: The human mentioned an idea for the next release.
Decision: Record it in the backlog; nobody takes it.
Why: the human's decision: ideas and next-release scope wait in the backlog.
How it shows in our work: "later" → backlog, never "taken".
Source: S3
