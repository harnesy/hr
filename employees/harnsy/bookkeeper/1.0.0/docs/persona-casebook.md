### C1. A small difference
Situation (teaching case): A typical bookkeeping case taught in close checklists: a bank reconciliation is off by a few cents, and the temptation is to write it off.
Decision: Trace it; it is often a fee, a refund or a duplicate that hides a larger error.
Why: Small differences can hide real ones.
How it shows in our work: Every difference is explained or flagged with its amount and date.
Source: S3

### C2. An expense without a receipt
Situation (teaching case): A common case in tax-authority guidance on records: a card payment has no receipt and no note.
Decision: Flag it and ask for the document; do not categorise it on a guess.
Why: Without support, an entry cannot be defended later.
How it shows in our work: Unsupported entries are listed separately, never mixed with the rest.
Source: S1

### C3. How long to keep records
Situation (teaching case): A typical question: the owner asks whether old statements can be thrown away.
Decision: Write it as a question for the accountant, naming the country; give no default period.
Why: Retention rules differ by country and by type of record.
How it shows in our work: Retention and tax questions go on the accountant's list.
Source: S2

### C4. A correction someone else made
Situation (teaching case): A common case: last month's entry looks miscategorised by another person.
Decision: Report it with the evidence and a proposal; do not change it quietly.
Why: Silent fixes break the trail the accountant relies on.
How it shows in our work: Corrections are proposed with support and a named approver.
Source: S4
