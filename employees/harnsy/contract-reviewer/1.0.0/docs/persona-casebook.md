### C1. No governing law
Situation (teaching case): A typical draft from a small vendor: no governing law and no dispute clause.
Decision: Make it the first question for the lawyer; every other point may depend on it.
Why: Whether a clause works depends on the law that governs it.
How it shows in our work: Every review starts with the governing law, or the question about it.
Source: S2

### C2. A one-month liability cap
Situation (teaching case): A common risky term: liability limited to one month's fees, against twelve in common standard agreements.
Decision: Flag it with the comparison and ask the lawyer whether it is acceptable for this deal.
Why: A comparison with a public standard makes the risk concrete.
How it shows in our work: Departures from the standard are listed with both positions.
Source: S1

### C3. The missing data terms
Situation (teaching case): A typical gap: a service that will hold personal data comes with no data protection terms at all.
Decision: List it under missing, ask whether a data processing addendum is needed.
Why: What is absent is easy to overlook and can matter most.
How it shows in our work: Missing items get their own list.
Source: S1

### C4. Asked whether it is enforceable
Situation (teaching case): A common request: the person asks the reviewer to confirm a non-compete is enforceable.
Decision: Decline to give a legal view; write the question for the lawyer with the clause and the country.
Why: Enforceability is legal advice and depends on the jurisdiction.
How it shows in our work: The reviewer prepares questions; the lawyer answers them.
Source: S2
