### C1. Name and price still moving
Situation: Teaching situation, a typical case from the source: the day before a release, the feature's name and price are still being discussed.
Decision: Let the product team release it; schedule the launch for when the name and price are settled.
Why: a launch built on a moving name means rebuilding the page, the video and the email.
How it shows in our work: the launch plan has a "settled" check for name, price and scope before any asset is made.
Source: S2

### C2. A feature missing from a pricing page
Situation: Teaching situation, a typical case from the source: a competitor's pricing page does not mention single sign-on, and a draft says they do not have it.
Decision: Write "absent from their pricing page on this date", or confirm in their documentation before claiming absence.
Why: "not seen" is not "does not exist"; a wrong claim on a comparison page costs trust and invites correction.
How it shows in our work: every competitor fact has a date and a place in the fact file.
Source: S1

### C3. A true objection
Situation: Teaching situation, a typical case from the source: buyers say a rival tool is cheaper at very high volume.
Decision: Concede that it is true at that volume, ask the buyer's real volume, then show what they would otherwise run as separate tools.
Why: denying a true point loses the buyer; conceding it earns the next question.
How it shows in our work: battle cards list objections with an honest first line and a follow-up question.
Source: S2

### C4. Old battle cards
Situation: Teaching situation, a typical case from the source: the comparison material is a year old and nobody knows which claims still hold.
Decision: Mark each claim current, changed, unverifiable or overclaimed; fix public pages first, then sales talk tracks, then internal notes; correct it once in the shared fact file.
Why: public pages do the most damage when wrong.
How it shows in our work: a dated audit table goes with every comparison refresh.
Source: S1

### C5. A customer story
Situation: Teaching situation, a typical case from the source: a small customer got a strong, measurable result and is willing to talk.
Decision: Prefer this story over a famous logo with no numbers; tell the account owner first; interview, draft quickly, send it to the customer to check facts and quotes before anyone publishes.
Why: the result convinces the reader; the customer's check protects them and us.
How it shows in our work: no story leaves draft without the customer's written OK.
Source: S2

### C6. A gifted product in a partner post
Situation: Teaching situation, a typical case from the source: a partner who got the product for free wants to post about it.
Decision: Brief them that the post must say plainly, next to the claim, that they got it free; give facts, never a script of opinions.
Why: a reader must know about the relationship to weigh the endorsement.
How it shows in our work: every partner brief carries the disclosure line.
Source: S5
