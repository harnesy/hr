# Goals draft

Good goals say what should be achieved, who is accountable, and how anyone will know it happened. This skill prepares them so the leader can set them quickly. It never sets, publishes or changes goals, and goals are never used here to rate people.

**Route:** the leader's larger goals and the planning date → **goals-draft** → the leader sets, edits and publishes → progress tracked in status reports → reflection at the end of the period.

## When to use

- A planning period (quarter, half-year, year) is about to start.
- A team asks for help shaping its goals so they feed the leader's.
- Not for performance reviews, ratings, promotions or pay: those are decisions for named people and out of scope.

## Inputs

- The leader's larger goals for the period, taken as given.
- Last period's goals and what happened to each.
- The contributors: who should give input, and by when.
- Data the motivations will cite (with source and date).
- The date by which the leader wants to set the goals.

## Steps

1. **Start from the leader's goals.** Write them at the top. Team goals feed them; a team may push back on them, and that pushback goes to the leader as written.
2. **Reflect on the last period.** For each old goal: met, partly met or missed, and why in a line. Carry nothing forward without saying why.
3. **Collect private written input.** Ask each contributor to write their ideas before any group session, and bring all input to the start of the session, so nobody anchors on the first voice.
4. **Draft a few objectives.** Each says what is to be achieved, in one sentence.
5. **One owner per objective.** A named person, not a team; the team can be listed as contributors.
6. **Motivation with a number.** Why this matters now, citing one number with its source and date.
7. **Two to four measurable results.** Each says how anyone will know the objective was met: a number, a date, a state that can be checked. Results are outcomes, not a task list.
8. **Label each objective** as a commitment (expected to be met in full) or a stretch (partial progress is a good result).
9. **Mark the whole document** «draft for <leader> to set». List open questions for the leader.

## Rules

- The agent drafts; the leader sets, edits and publishes. Nothing is published or shared beyond the leader without the leader's word.
- Goals are never used by the agent to score, rank or judge people.
- No method or framework is named; the draft speaks of objectives and measurable results.

## Checks before hand-over

- The leader's goals are at the top, unchanged.
- Every objective has one named owner, a motivation with a cited number, two to four measurable results and a label.
- No result is just a task.
- The document is marked as a draft for the leader to set.

## Artefacts

- `goals/<period>/draft.md`: leader's goals, reflection, objectives, owners, results, labels, open questions.
- `goals/<period>/input.md`: private written input as received, kept for the leader only.

## Hand-over

- **To the leader:** the draft and open questions. The leader sets, edits and publishes.
- **To owners, after the leader sets them:** their objectives, from the leader.
- **To status reports:** the measurable results, to track progress.
