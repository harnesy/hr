# Meeting brief

A brief lets the person (the principal: a leader, a manager, a client lead, anyone who attends or chairs) walk into a meeting knowing why it exists, who will be there, and what must be decided. It is written for that one reader and fits on a page or two.

**Route:** meeting on the calendar or a request from the principal → **meeting-brief** → the principal reads it → the meeting → decisions go to `decision-log`, actions to their owners.

## When to use

- Before any meeting the principal attends or chairs: leadership, board, client, partner, one-to-one, project review.
- When the principal asks «what do I need to know for this?».
- Not for booking rooms, sending invitations or travel: those are logistics for the person or assistant who keeps the calendar.

## Inputs

- The meeting: title, date, length, the invitation text and agenda if any.
- Who invited whom, and the principal's role in it (chair, decider, presenter, guest).
- Prior notes from the same series, the open action list, recent threads and documents linked to the topics.
- How much time the principal has to read (two minutes or twenty).

## Steps

1. **Identify the meeting.** Its type, its purpose, and the outcome the principal wants from it. If nobody can say what the meeting is for, write that first: it may not need to happen.
2. **List the participants.** For each: role, what they care about in this meeting, and anything open between them and the principal.
3. **Gather.** Prior meeting notes, open actions from last time with their owners, related documents and recent threads. Read them; do not just link them.
4. **Write the brief:**
   - purpose and wanted outcome, in two lines;
   - participants and their interests;
   - short background: only what the principal needs for this meeting;
   - open issues, each in one or two lines;
   - questions the principal may want to raise;
   - decisions needed: for each, the options, a recommendation with its reason, and the date by which it must be decided;
   - follow-ups due from last time, with owners;
   - **gaps:** what could not be found or checked, and who might know.
5. **Fit the reading time.** Put the decisions first if the principal has two minutes; cut background before cutting decisions or gaps.
6. **After the meeting,** if asked: decisions to `decision-log`, actions to their owners with dates.

## Rules

- The brief informs; it never speaks for the principal. Recommendations are marked as recommendations; the principal or the named decider decides.
- Nothing is sent to participants from the brief unless the principal says so.
- Private matters about participants (health, family, other personal details) stay out of the brief.

## Checks before hand-over

- Purpose and wanted outcome are stated.
- Every decision needed has options, a recommendation and a deadline.
- Every open action has an owner.
- Gaps are listed, not hidden.
- It fits the reading time the principal has.

## Artefacts

- `briefs/<YYYY-MM-DD>-<meeting>.md`: the brief.

## Hand-over

- **To the principal:** the brief, ahead of the meeting.
- **To whoever keeps the calendar** (an executive assistant or the principal): any logistics the brief turned up.
- **To `decision-log` and action owners:** decisions and actions after the meeting.
