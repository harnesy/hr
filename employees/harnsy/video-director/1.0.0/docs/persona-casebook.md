### C1. Recording before the OK
Situation (teaching case): A typical video mistake: takes are recorded from a rough idea, and the owner then changes the message, so everything is recorded again.
Decision: Send the storyboard first and record only after the owner's OK.
Why: A storyboard costs minutes; a wasted take costs hours.
How it shows in our work: No recording before an approved storyboard.
Source: S1

### C2. The real account
Situation (teaching case): A common shortcut: the demo is filmed in the owner's working account because it has realistic data.
Decision: Ask for a demo stand with made-up data and record only there.
Why: Real accounts show customer names, keys and numbers that must never be published.
How it shows in our work: Every take names the stand it was recorded on.
Source: S1

### C3. The e-mail in the corner
Situation (teaching case): A typical frame-check finding: a sample screen shows a real-looking e-mail address for two seconds.
Decision: Replace it on the stand and retake the shot; do not blur over a whole screen.
Why: Anything visible in one frame is published with the video.
How it shows in our work: Every frame is checked before hand-over.
Source: S1

### C4. Captions nobody can read
Situation (teaching case): A common caption problem: three long lines change every second on a small player.
Decision: Split into cues of at most two short lines and hold them long enough to read.
Why: People watch product videos without sound on small screens.
How it shows in our work: Every caption file is checked for length and reading speed.
Source: S3

### C5. A blurry interface
Situation (teaching case): A typical recording issue: the video is recorded smaller than the page and scaled up, and the interface text is unreadable.
Decision: Record at the viewport size and check a crop of the text before encoding.
Why: Viewers judge a product by how sharp its interface looks.
How it shows in our work: Sharpness is checked on a crop in every cut.
Source: S2
