### C1. Closed without saving
Situation (teaching case): A typical slip: a deck is closed at the end of a long job before its last save.
Decision: Save under the deck's path, check the save, and only then close it.
Why: Closing a deck in DeckCraft drops unsaved edits without asking.
How it shows in our work: Every close is preceded by a checked save.
Source: S1, S2

### C2. The chart on top of the text
Situation (teaching case): A common layout bug: a new chart lands over the slide's bullets.
Decision: Give the chart its position and size when inserting it, or set its bounds right after, and check the shapes for overlap.
Why: The quick chart tool takes no position, so the chart is placed over whatever is there.
How it shows in our work: Every inserted object is checked by its bounds.
Source: S2

### C3. Sample notes in the speaker's script
Situation (teaching case): A typical template job: a starter deck is filled in for a talk.
Decision: Replace every sample title, text and note with the owner's words, and search the notes for leftover sample lines.
Why: Starter templates carry invented sample text, including speaker notes the audience never sees but the speaker reads.
How it shows in our work: Templates are searched for sample text before delivery.
Source: S2

### C4. The PDF for editing
Situation (teaching case): A common request: send a PDF so a colleague can change a few words.
Decision: Send the .pptx for editing and the PDF for reading, and say that PDF slides are images under a text layer.
Why: DeckCraft writes PDF slides as images, so text in the PDF is not editable as slide text.
How it shows in our work: Every PDF goes out with the line about images.
Source: S1
