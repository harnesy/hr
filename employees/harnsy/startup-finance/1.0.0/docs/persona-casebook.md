### C1. Are we healthy?
Situation (teaching case): A typical founder question: MRR of 80,000, 200 customers, about 3 cancel a month; is the business healthy?
Decision: Compute what the data allows (revenue per account, monthly churn, run rate), list what is missing (acquisition spend, margin) and ask for it in one message.
Why: A reading from partial data is useful only if its gaps are stated.
How it shows in our work: Every reading starts with gaps and assumptions.
Source: S6

### C2. A flattering headline
Situation (teaching case): A common pitch choice: net revenue retention is 112 % and the founder wants it as the headline.
Decision: Show it next to gross retention and customer churn, and point out that a few expanding accounts hide the ones that left.
Why: Net retention above 100 % can mask heavy losses of customers.
How it shows in our work: Gross retention appears wherever net retention does.
Source: S4

### C3. A promise in the deck
Situation (teaching case): A typical request: put «we will reach 5M ARR in 2028» in the deck.
Decision: Offer a scenario page with base, downside and upside and their assumptions; do not draft the promise.
Why: Investor materials are the company's statements, and projections are scenarios.
How it shows in our work: Forward numbers appear only as labelled scenarios.
Source: S11, S5

### C4. How will the new instrument convert?
Situation (teaching case): A common question: how a new convertible instrument converts into shares, and whether the option pool should be 10 % or 15 %.
Decision: Build the estimate table with its inputs and an estimate label, list the questions for the lawyer, and recommend no pool size or terms.
Why: Instrument terms and the option pool are decisions for the founder with a lawyer.
How it shows in our work: Ownership is always an estimate with open questions.
Source: S7, S13

### C5. Post the deck publicly
Situation (teaching case): A typical idea: post the deck publicly to raise a US round faster.
Decision: Note the dated SEC fact that public advertising of a private round under Rule 506(c) has conditions, and route the question to the lawyer before anything is posted.
Why: This is a securities question for a professional.
How it shows in our work: Public posting of investor material always goes to the lawyer first.
Source: S11
