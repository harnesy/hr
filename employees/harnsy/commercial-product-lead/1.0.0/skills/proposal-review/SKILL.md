# Proposal review

Teams bring ideas: a new headline, a pricing table, a trial button, a tracking plan, a campaign. This skill gives each one a verdict against the agreed problem: accept, reframe or stop, always with the reason. It reviews the work and never the person, and it never rewrites the proposal; the proposer does.

**Route:** the agreed page from `commercial-problem` + a proposal → **proposal-review** → accepted ideas go onto a card in `hypothesis-backlog`.

## When to use

- A team member or another role brings one or more proposed changes to the site, the offer, marketing or the sales route.
- A plan or report counts activity (pages shipped, posts sent, sign-ups) as success.
- Not for auditing a page for usability or running a test (the conversion optimization specialist does that), and not for reviewing an investor pitch.

## Inputs

- The agreed problem page. Without one, every verdict is «stop: the problem comes first», with an offer to draft it.
- The proposals, each as the proposer wrote it.
- The numbers already known, with dates; read-only.

## Steps

1. **Own view first.** Read only the change itself, the problem page and the evidence, and write a first verdict before reading the proposer's justification or anyone else's opinion. Then read them and note where they change your view, and why.
2. **Problem and audience.** Which line of the problem page does it serve, and for which buyer and market? None: say so.
3. **Chain to paying clients.** Write it out: what changes → what the buyer does differently → what that does to paid orders. Mark the link that is only assumed, and grade the evidence for it, strongest first: what buyers did (orders, requests followed to payment), what buyers told us, published data with source and date, a rival's practice, the team's own view, and last, simulated buyers (an AI playing a buyer), which suggest questions but are no buyers.
4. **Measure and date.** Which number would show it worked, its starting value, and the date by which it should move. If the measure is an early step (sign-ups, demo views, pilot requests, clicks), add the later step that shows a paid order.
5. **What it runs ahead of.** Copy, buttons, forms, flows and tracking proposed before a hypothesis is chosen; name the step that must come first.
6. **Harm check.** Who could this hurt, and how: buyers misled, trust lost, more load on sales or support, personal data collected without need.
7. **Verdict.**
   - **Accept:** it serves a stated problem, the chain is written and the measure has a date. It goes onto a hypothesis card.
   - **Reframe:** the idea points at a real problem but asks the wrong question; write the question it should answer instead.
   - **Stop:** it runs ahead of the problem, has no link to paying clients, or fails the harm check; give the reason and what must come first, and what would reopen it.
8. **Hand back** the table to the proposer and the owner.

## Rules

- Verdicts are about the work: each names the proposal, the missing piece and the exact change asked for; nothing about the person.
- You do not rewrite the proposal and you write no copy; the proposer revises it.
- Tricks are stopped whatever effect is expected: false urgency, invented scarcity, made-up reviews or client logos, a hidden price, a form designed to mislead.
- A stop is not forever: it says what would reopen it.
- A rival's page is never accepted as a model to copy; it is evidence for a hypothesis at most, and a rival selling well is no proof that its wording works.

## Checks before hand-over

- Each verdict was written before the proposer's case was read, and any change after reading has its reason.
- Every proposal has a chain to paying clients with the assumed link marked and its evidence graded; no accept rests only on the team's own view or on simulated buyers.
- No proposal is accepted with an early step as its only measure.
- Every reframe has the new question written out; every stop has its reason, what comes first and what would reopen it.
- No verdict comments on a person.

## Artefacts

- `commercial/reviews-<date>.md`: one row per proposal with proposer, problem and audience, chain to paying clients with the evidence grade, measure with start value and date, what it runs ahead of, harm, verdict, and the reason, new question or first step.

## Hand-over

- **To the proposer and the owner:** the table.
- **To `hypothesis-backlog`:** each accepted proposal, as a draft card.
- **To the product marketing manager:** a finding that the message or positioning is the problem, for them to rewrite.
- **To the owner:** anything that touches prices, contracts, personal data or claims about rivals.
