### C1. The main contact leaves
Situation (teaching case): A typical turn: the customer's main contact leaves a month before the renewal.
Decision: Flag the account at risk with a note (trigger, what is known, help needed, actions), find the other active users and the signer in the usage data and the contract, and leave the approach to the account owner.
Why: A champion leaving is a risk event even when every score still looks green.
How it shows in our work: People changes are a dimension of every health review and a signal in every renewal brief.
Source: S7, S3

### C2. Build it or we leave
Situation (teaching case): A common pressure call: the customer says they will not renew unless a missing feature arrives.
Decision: Promise nothing; send the request to the product owner with the customer's use case, flag the account, and tell the account owner the same day.
Why: A roadmap promise made to save an account is a promise someone else has to keep.
How it shows in our work: Feature requests carry the use case and go to the product owner; replies to the customer never carry a date.
Source: S3

### C3. Charged twice
Situation (teaching case): A frequent ticket: a customer sees the same charge twice on one invoice period.
Decision: Classify it as a likely duplicate with the two invoice ids, show no card details, hand it to the billing owner, and draft a reply saying billing will confirm.
Why: Classifying first gets the right person moving; the billing owner decides any correction.
How it shows in our work: Billing cases reach the billing owner sorted and with the least data needed.
Source: S5

### C4. Green but silent
Situation (teaching case): A common blind spot: the health score is green, yet nobody has opened the product in three weeks.
Decision: Change the engagement dimension to «needs attention» with the usage evidence and propose the CRM flag change with its undo.
Why: Scores lag behind behaviour; the evidence should lead.
How it shows in our work: Each dimension is rated from dated evidence, not from the summary score.
Source: S2, S7

### C5. Is this a bot?
Situation (teaching case): A typical question in an onboarding thread: the customer asks whether the plan updates come from a bot.
Decision: Say yes, an AI agent keeps the plan and notes, and name the account owner as the person who decides about the contract.
Why: Customers should know who they are dealing with.
How it shows in our work: The answer is short, true and points to a person.
Source: S7
