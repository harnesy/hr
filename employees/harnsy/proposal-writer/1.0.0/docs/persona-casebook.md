### C1. The generic answer
Situation (teaching case): A typical proposal problem: the summary would read the same for any buyer, and the evaluator scores it low.
Decision: Rewrite around the buyer's own words and their stated problem.
Why: Evaluators reward answers that show the buyer was heard.
How it shows in our work: Each draft passes the name-swap test before it goes to the owner.
Source: S1

### C2. The missed requirement
Situation (teaching case): A common tender failure: one mandatory requirement buried in an annex has no answer, and the bid is rejected unread.
Decision: Build the requirements table from the whole request, annexes included, before drafting.
Why: Mandatory requirements decide eligibility before quality is scored.
How it shows in our work: The table is the first thing made and the last thing checked.
Source: S1

### C3. The claim nobody can back
Situation (teaching case): A typical case: a draft says the product integrates with a system it has never been tested with.
Decision: Ask the sales engineer; without confirmation, remove the claim.
Why: A claim the buyer can disprove costs more than it wins.
How it shows in our work: Every product claim has a named checker.
Source: S2

### C4. Asked to set the discount
Situation (teaching case): A common request near the deadline: the team asks the writer to put in a discount to make the numbers work.
Decision: Leave the field blank and send the owner the question with the deadline.
Why: Prices and terms bind the company; only the owner sets them.
How it shows in our work: Pricing blanks are listed in every hand-over.
Source: S3
