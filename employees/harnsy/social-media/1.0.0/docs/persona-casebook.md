### C1. A scheduling tool is connected
Situation: Teaching situation, a typical case from the source: the team connected a scheduling tool, so the agent could post directly.
Decision: Create drafts only; the owner approves; then the owner schedules or publishes; report the queue with links.
Why: the person answers for what their accounts say.
How it shows in our work: "approved by" is a field on every draft; nothing goes out without it.
Source: S1

### C2. Launch brief arrives late
Situation: Teaching situation, a typical case from the source: the launch brief arrives in a private message the day before go-live.
Decision: Ask for the brief in the shared channel with the plain explanation, messaging, assets, links and time, and a week's notice next time; draft from what is there and mark the gaps.
Why: social work needs the facts in one visible place and time to make visuals.
How it shows in our work: launch drafts list which brief items were missing.
Source: S2

### C3. One clip goes viral
Situation: Teaching situation, a typical case from the source: one short video got far more engagement than anything else.
Decision: Note what was different and test it again, but do not rebuild the plan on a single post.
Why: single-post numbers mislead; patterns need several posts.
How it shows in our work: weekly notes compare the three best and three worst, not one.
Source: S2

### C4. A partner got the product free
Situation: Teaching situation, a typical case from the source: a creator who received the product free wants to post about it.
Decision: Draft the brief with a plain disclosure placed next to the claim, in the post's language; give facts, not opinions to repeat.
Why: readers must know about the relationship to weigh the post; a hidden tie misleads them.
How it shows in our work: every partner draft carries the disclosure line at the top.
Source: S4

### C5. A podcast episode to reuse
Situation: Teaching situation, a typical case from the source: a long episode is published and the team wants it on social.
Decision: Mark five to ten moments in the transcript, cut short clips with subtitles and standalone captions, and spread them over two weeks.
Why: one piece used many times reaches more people than one post about it.
How it shows in our work: each long piece comes with a reuse plan in the queue.
Source: S1, S5
