# Source log

Research that cannot be repeated cannot be checked. A source log records every search and every document as you go, with dates, so that another person can see what was looked at, what was found, what was missed, and how old each piece of evidence is.

**Route:** any desk research → **source-log** (kept as you go) → travels with every report, to whoever checks or relies on it.

## When to use

- Any research whose results someone will rely on: patent and literature searches, tax or legal research, market and competitor research, fact checks.
- When research is updated later: the update is added to the same log.

## Inputs

- The research question and its date.
- Any limits set by the person who asked (sources allowed, date range, budget, confidentiality).

## Steps

1. **Open the log** with the question, who asked, the date, and any limits (for example «unpublished material: neutral terms only, owner's written OK on 9 October»).
2. **One row per search,** written when it runs: date and time; database, catalogue or site; the exact query, code or path; filters (dates, languages, document types); number of hits; how many were screened.
3. **One row per document kept:** title; author or publisher; its own publication date, or «undated»; the access date; the link, number or shelf mark; why it was kept; where in it the relevant part is.
4. **Dates of online items.** An item counts from when it was posted, if that can be shown. A page with no date cannot date itself: log it as «undated» and look for other evidence, such as an archived capture whose timestamp shows the page existed by then, or a dated citation by someone else. Timestamps on social media and on pages that are edited later need care; say what the date shows and what it does not.
5. **Limits.** When a search is limited (one database, one language, free sources only, a date range), say so in the row.
6. **Updates add, never overwrite.** A later search or a correction is a new row that points to the old one.
7. **Close the log** with what was searched, what was not, and the date of the last search.

## Rules

- **Write as you go,** not from memory afterwards.
- **Append only.** No row is edited or deleted; corrections are new rows.
- **Exact queries,** copied as run.
- **Every document has two dates:** its own (or «undated») and the access date.
- **Confidential material** is never pasted into the log in full; the log records that it was used and where it is kept.

## Checks before hand-over

- Every search has a date, a source, an exact query and a hit count.
- Every document kept has its own date or «undated», an access date and a link or number.
- Limits are written down.
- The log ends with what was not searched.

## Artefacts

- `<project>/search-log.md` (or a spreadsheet with the same columns): header, search rows, document rows, closing note.

## Hand-over

- **With every report** that relies on the research, to its reader: for example a patent attorney, a tax adviser, a lawyer, a marketing strategist or an editor.
- **To whoever updates the research next:** the log, so the new rows continue it.
