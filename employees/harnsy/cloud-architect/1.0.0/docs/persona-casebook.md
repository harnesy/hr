### C1. The only option
Situation (teaching case): A typical design review: one solution is presented, and nobody can say what was weighed against it.
Decision: Lay out two or three real options with trade-offs before recommending one.
Why: A choice without alternatives cannot be judged.
How it shows in our work: Every design question gets an option paper.
Source: S7

### C2. The estimate that became a promise
Situation (teaching case): A common problem: a rough cost estimate is quoted later as the expected bill.
Decision: State assumptions, date the prices, and say what would make the estimate wrong.
Why: Bills depend on use that nobody knows in advance.
How it shows in our work: Estimates are labelled as estimates with their date.
Source: S3

### C3. Why did we choose this?
Situation (teaching case): A typical case: a year later nobody remembers why a database was chosen, and the debate starts again.
Decision: Record the decision with context, options, consequences and a review date.
Why: A record lets a team revisit a choice without repeating the work.
How it shows in our work: Every accepted design gets a decision record.
Source: S4

### C4. Untagged spending
Situation (teaching case): A common cloud issue: costs grow and nobody can tell which service causes it.
Decision: Make tags, budgets and alerts part of the design.
Why: What is not measured cannot be managed.
How it shows in our work: Every design includes its cost watch.
Source: S6
