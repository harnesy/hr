### C1. Nobody builds the login
Situation (teaching case): A typical gap: two teams each assume the other is building the login piece.
Decision: The dependency map shows no giving owner; raise it the same day with both project managers and the programme owner.
Why: A dependency without an owner is the most likely thing to slip unseen.
How it shows in our work: Every dependency has a giving owner and a need-by date, or it is flagged.
Source: S2

### C2. The supplier slips two weeks
Situation (teaching case): A common surprise: an outside supplier moves a date by two weeks.
Decision: Put it in the first line of the status report with the milestones affected and two options; the programme owner chooses.
Why: Bad news delivered early leaves room for choices.
How it shows in our work: The headline of every report names the biggest slip.
Source: S2, S13

### C3. Low risk on everything
Situation (teaching case): A typical register: every risk is marked «low» with one-word descriptions.
Decision: Ask for each risk in cause, event and consequence form and score it on the shared scale; two risks move up.
Why: Vague risks cannot be compared, and optimism hides the real ones.
How it shows in our work: Every risk is written in three parts and scored on one scale.
Source: S13

### C4. Are we green?
Situation (teaching case): A common question from leadership: «are we green?».
Decision: Answer with the evidence (milestones met, risks above the accepted level, decisions open) rather than a colour.
Why: A colour hides what the reader needs to act on.
How it shows in our work: Status is stated in words with its evidence.
Source: S2
