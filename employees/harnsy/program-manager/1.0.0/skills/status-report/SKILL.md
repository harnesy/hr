# Status report

A status report lets a busy reader know in a minute whether things are on track, what is wrong, and what they must decide. It is written from evidence, it states the state in words (not only colours), and it brings bad news early.

**Route:** plan, dependencies and risks (from `plan-and-milestones`, `dependency-map`, `risk-log`) and team updates → **status-report** → the owner or leadership reads and decides → decisions to `decision-log`.

## When to use

- Before each regular status date (weekly, fortnightly, monthly).
- As soon as something important slips, without waiting for the date.
- When someone asks «are we on track?».

## Inputs

- The approved plan and its milestones.
- Updates from each team, with the date of each update.
- The dependency map and the risk log, including the level of risk leadership accepts.
- Open decisions and who must make them.

## Steps

1. **Headline.** One or two sentences: the overall state (on track, at risk, off track) in words, and the single most important thing the reader should know.
2. **Target against actual.** Milestones and any agreed measures: planned date or value, actual or current forecast, and the source of each.
3. **Done and in progress.** Short lists; every item has an owner and a date.
4. **Risks and issues.** The few that matter now, each with owner and response; say whether each sits inside the level leadership accepts, and whether the responses are working.
5. **Decisions needed.** For each: the question, the options, a recommendation with its reason, who decides and by when.
6. **Next priorities.** What the next period focuses on.
7. **Bad news first.** If a milestone will slip, it goes in the headline with its effect and the options.
8. **Read it as the reader.** Cut anything that does not help them act. Spell out every abbreviation once.

## Rules

- State comes from evidence (dates, measures, updates), not from mood. Colours, if used, always come with words.
- Other teams' and suppliers' dates are reported as their owners gave them, with when.
- Nothing measured is promised in advance.
- The report informs and recommends; decisions are made by named people.

## Checks before hand-over

- The headline states the overall state in words.
- Every item has an owner and a date; every figure has a source.
- Every decision needed has options, a recommendation, a decider and a deadline.
- Risks say whether they sit inside the accepted level.

## Artefacts

- `status/<YYYY-MM-DD>.md`: the report.

## Hand-over

- **To the owner or leadership:** the report and the decisions needed.
- **To the person who keeps the leader's agenda** (a chief of staff, where there is one): decisions that need the leader.
- **To `decision-log`:** decisions once made.
