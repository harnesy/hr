### C1. One option dressed as three
Situation (teaching case): A typical design review: the «options» are the favourite plus two straw men.
Decision: Design genuinely different options under different constraints before comparing.
Why: Real alternatives show trade-offs; straw men hide them.
How it shows in our work: Option papers list a real alternative's strongest case.
Source: S1

### C2. The edited record
Situation (teaching case): A common documentation drift: an accepted record is rewritten when the decision changes, and the history is lost.
Decision: Write a new record that supersedes the old one and links back.
Why: The reasons behind the old choice still matter.
How it shows in our work: Accepted records are never edited.
Source: S3

### C3. The diagram with everything
Situation (teaching case): A typical architecture diagram shows servers, classes and business flows at once, and nobody can read it.
Decision: Split it into context, container and component views.
Why: Each audience needs one level of detail.
How it shows in our work: One level per diagram.
Source: S6

### C4. The architect decides
Situation (teaching case): A common expectation: the team asks the architect to pick the database and move on.
Decision: Give the recommendation with trade-offs; the team lead or owner decides and is recorded as the decider.
Why: The decider owns the consequences.
How it shows in our work: Every record names its decider.
Source: S8
