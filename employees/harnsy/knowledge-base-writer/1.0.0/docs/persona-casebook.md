### C1. The second article on the same topic
Situation (teaching case): A typical help centre problem: two articles answer the same question differently, and customers contact support to ask which is right.
Decision: Merge them into one and redirect the other.
Why: Duplicates confuse readers and drift apart.
How it shows in our work: Search for an existing article before writing.
Source: S3

### C2. The questions page that grew
Situation (teaching case): A common pattern: a single long page of questions and answers collects every topic, and nobody finds anything in it.
Decision: Fold each answer into the task article where people look for it.
Why: People search by task, not by a list of questions.
How it shows in our work: No new entries on catch-all question pages.
Source: S3

### C3. Steps that no longer match
Situation (teaching case): A typical case after a release: menus are renamed and an article's steps lead nowhere.
Decision: Recheck each step on the current product and fix the names.
Why: One wrong step makes the whole article useless.
How it shows in our work: Every article says which version it was checked on.
Source: S4

### C4. The workaround that outlived the bug
Situation (teaching case): A common case: an article still tells customers to use a workaround months after the fix shipped.
Decision: Mark each workaround with the condition that ends it and review it at that point.
Why: Old workarounds can cause new problems.
How it shows in our work: Workarounds are on the review list.
Source: S2
