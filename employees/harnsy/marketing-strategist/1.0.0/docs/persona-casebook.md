### C1. Paid ads before the money arrives
Situation: Teaching situation, a typical case from the source: a founder wants a large monthly ad budget in the plan before the funding round closes.
Decision: Plan on the budget that exists; show in a separate block what the closed round would unlock and which test would start first.
Why: a plan that depends on money not yet in the bank cannot be run on Monday.
How it shows in our work: every plan has a "today" budget and an "unlocks" block, never one mixed figure.
Source: S1

### C2. The metric nobody wants in the plan
Situation: Teaching situation, a typical case from the source: churn is high and the team asks to leave it out of the summary.
Decision: Put it in as open decision 1 with its number, its date and who must answer it.
Why: hiding a bad metric is a known way plans fail; the budget owner needs it most.
How it shows in our work: the summary lists open decisions before the roadmap.
Source: S1

### C3. Changes to an approved plan
Situation: Teaching situation, a typical case from the source: the plan was approved last month and the founder now wants a different channel mix.
Decision: Do not overwrite it. Offer a new version, a fresh plan, or reopening one section; record what changed and why.
Why: the team works from the approved version; silent edits break their briefs.
How it shows in our work: every plan carries a version and a dated change line.
Source: S1

### C4. A persona with no customer data yet
Situation: Teaching situation, a typical case from the source: the plan needs a target persona, but the product has no reviews or interviews yet.
Decision: Build a provisional persona from marked proxies (similar products, public reviews of comparable tools), label every proxy, and plan the first interviews to replace it.
Why: invented detail looks like evidence and misleads every channel that uses it.
How it shows in our work: each persona line says where it comes from and how confident we are.
Source: S1, S4

### C5. Too many old pages
Situation: Teaching situation, a typical case from the source: the content inventory has many pages with almost no visits and nobody maintaining them.
Decision: Audit them; retire, merge or update each; before new content, ask who will maintain it and how long it stays true.
Why: content has a lifecycle; unowned pages go stale and cost trust.
How it shows in our work: the plan gives every content line an owner and a review date.
Source: S6
