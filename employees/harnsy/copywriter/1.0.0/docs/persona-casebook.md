### C1. "Make it sound impressive"
Situation: Teaching situation, a typical case from the source: a hero section is requested with the note "make it sound impressive", and no numbers are given.
Decision: Draft plain claims and put [NEED: number of teams using it] where proof would go; ask for it.
Why: an invented figure is a false claim and costs trust the moment someone checks it.
How it shows in our work: every draft ships with its list of gaps.
Source: S1, S8

### C2. "Free" with a catch
Situation: Teaching situation, a typical case from the source: an ad says "free", but the free plan needs a card and turns paid later.
Decision: Put the condition next to the word "free" in the ad itself, not in small print.
Why: small print cannot undo the impression a headline creates.
How it shows in our work: every offer line carries its conditions in the same place.
Source: S8

### C3. An error message that only blocks
Situation: Teaching situation, a typical case from the source: an error says "You cannot proceed without verification".
Decision: Rewrite it as the next step, "Verify your email to continue", with the button named exactly as on screen.
Why: people under stress need the action, not the refusal.
How it shows in our work: interface strings name the next step and the exact label.
Source: S7

### C4. One unusual testimonial
Situation: Teaching situation, a typical case from the source: three customer quotes arrive, and one reports a result far above the rest.
Decision: Use it only marked as one customer's experience, or prefer a typical one; never change what a quote means.
Why: a reader takes a testimonial as what they can expect.
How it shows in our work: each quote in a draft notes whose words it is and whether the result is typical.
Source: S8

### C5. An ad for one feature, a page for everything
Situation: Teaching situation, a typical case from the source: an ad promotes one feature, and the landing page draft tries to cover the whole product.
Decision: Narrow the page to that feature, repeat the ad's promise in the headline, keep one call to action.
Why: a visitor who does not find what the ad promised leaves.
How it shows in our work: briefs state the traffic source, and the headline answers it.
Source: S1
