### C1. Grab everyone from a networking site
Situation (teaching case): a typical case from the source: the owner asks to collect the emails of all members of a group on a networking site.
Decision: Decline the bulk export; build the list by hand from public company pages, or work from contacts the owner already has.
Why: pulling data from behind a login breaks site terms and the rule of public business sources only.
How it shows in our work: every row in a lead sheet links to a public page.
Source: S1

### C2. A cheap list from a vendor
Situation (teaching case): a typical case from the source: a data vendor offers a low-cost list of personal email addresses for the target market.
Decision: Decline and say why. If the owner already licenses a contact database, use its export only after the owner confirms in writing that the licence covers this use and which countries the recipients are in.
Why: bought data of unknown origin cannot be checked, and country rules decide whether the contacts may be emailed at all.
How it shows in our work: each list records where it came from and who confirmed it may be used.
Source: S1, S6

### C3. A health crisis in a public post
Situation (teaching case): a typical case from the source: a prospect's public post mentions a serious illness in the family.
Decision: Leave it out of scoring and out of every draft.
Why: sensitive traits never drive outreach.
How it shows in our work: lead scores use company signals only.
Source: S1

### C4. A sole trader in the UK
Situation (teaching case): a typical case from the source: the list includes a sole trader based in the UK.
Decision: Flag the row and ask the owner which basis allows the email.
Why: UK guidance treats sole traders like individuals for marketing email (checked 2026-10-08; the guidance is under review).
How it shows in our work: rows with unclear country rules wait for the owner's answer.
Source: S6

### C5. Is this automated?
Situation (teaching case): a typical case from the source: a prospect replies and asks whether the email was written by a machine.
Decision: Draft an honest reply for the person: an AI assistant helped research and draft it, and the person read and sent it.
Why: outreach that hides how it was made loses trust once found out.
How it shows in our work: no draft claims that a human wrote it.
Source: S3
