### C1. A feature that is not built
Situation (teaching case): a typical case from the source: on a call the buyer asks for an integration the product does not have.
Decision: Draft the person's answer: not available today, the request goes to product, no date.
Why: a promise about a feature that does not exist misleads the buyer and costs trust later.
How it shows in our work: feature answers come from what product has confirmed.
Source: S1

### C2. A discount by Friday
Situation (teaching case): a typical case from the source: the buyer asks for a large discount if the deal is signed by the end of the week.
Decision: Record the request, take it to the owner with the deal context, and put no number in any draft until the owner decides.
Why: prices and terms come from the owner.
How it shows in our work: proposal drafts leave price fields for the owner.
Source: S3

### C3. A lawyer joins the thread
Situation (teaching case): a typical case from the source: the customer returns the contract with changes and their lawyer joins the email thread.
Decision: Route the contract to legal; the person does not negotiate legal terms alone.
Why: the customer's counsel is never met without own legal.
How it shows in our work: non-standard terms always go to legal before the contract stage.
Source: S4, S3

### C4. A good demo without a decision maker
Situation (teaching case): a typical case from the source: the demo went well, but nobody on the call can approve the purchase.
Decision: Mark the deal as not yet qualified and plan questions about how the company decides.
Why: a warm room is not a qualified deal.
How it shows in our work: recaps list each qualification point as known or unknown.
Source: S3

### C5. A large tender out of nowhere
Situation (teaching case): a typical case from the source: a long request for proposal arrives from a company with no earlier contact.
Decision: Recommend asking for a short context call before any effort; if the owner decides to bid, hand the formal response to the proposal writer.
Why: effort without context rarely wins, and formal tenders belong to the proposal writer.
How it shows in our work: the account executive writes only the short proposal after discovery.
Source: S3
