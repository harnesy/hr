# Learning objectives

Objectives are the contract of a course: they say what learners will be able to do afterwards, in a form someone can check. Everything else (content, practice, quizzes) is built from them.

**Route:** request from a manager, team lead, HR generalist or onboarding specialist → **learning-objectives** → the requester confirms → `course-outline` → `quiz-build` where a section needs one.

## When to use

- Someone asks for a course, a workshop, a training or «something so people understand X».
- An existing course gets poor feedback and nobody can say what it was meant to achieve.
- Not when the problem is a process, a tool or an incentive: say so and name the owner instead.

## Inputs

- The request, in the requester's words.
- Who the learners are, and two or three of them to talk to if possible.
- How much time learners can give (one hour, a half day, several sessions).
- Who confirms the content (a subject expert).

## Steps

1. **Is training the answer?** Ask what goes wrong today and why. If people know what to do but the tool, the process or the incentives stop them, write that down and hand it to the owner. Continue only for a gap in skill or knowledge.
2. **Describe the learners.** In a few lines: what they do now, what they can already do, what they want to manage after the course, and what problem the course solves for them. Write one or two typical learners if the group is mixed.
3. **List prerequisites.** What learners must already be able to do. Add three or four short questions that tell whether someone fits.
4. **Name likely misconceptions.** Wrong ideas learners may bring from similar tools, older processes or habits. These feed the quiz options later.
5. **Write course objectives.** Each finishes the sentence «By the end, the learner can…» with one action someone could watch (list, choose, explain, fix, build, submit) and one skill. Replace «know», «understand» and «be aware of» with what a learner would do to show it.
6. **Write section objectives.** Narrower ones under each course objective, small enough for one section.
7. **Be realistic.** Check the list against the time available; cut objectives rather than rushing them.
8. **Tie each objective to a check.** For each one, write how a learner will show it (a practice task, a short question, a discussion prompt). The check is designed now, before any content.

## Checks before hand-over

- Each objective has one action someone could watch and one skill.
- No objective uses «know», «understand» or similar.
- Each objective has a planned check.
- Prerequisites and fit questions are listed.
- The total fits the time available.
- Any non-training cause is written down with its owner.

## Artefacts

- `learning/<course>/learners.md`: learner profile, prerequisites, fit questions, misconceptions.
- `learning/<course>/objectives.md`: course and section objectives, each with its planned check.

## Hand-over

- **To the requester:** the learner profile and objectives, to confirm before any content is written.
- **To the owner of a non-training cause:** the description of the problem.
- **To `course-outline`:** the confirmed objectives.
