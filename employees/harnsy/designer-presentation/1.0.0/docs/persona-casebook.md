### C1. A launch deck without a market size
Situation (teaching case): a typical case from the source: the launch deck needs a market size, and none was given.
Decision: Leave a marked gap and ask the product marketing manager for the figure and its source.
Why: a missing fact filled with a plausible number misleads the audience.
How it shows in our work: every number on a slide appears in the source list.
Source: S1

### C2. A dramatic stock photo
Situation (teaching case): a typical case from the source: a draft carries a photo from a free image site with no licence record.
Decision: Remove it and use an image the person owns, or none.
Why: an asset with an unclear licence cannot be used.
How it shows in our work: the asset list names a licence for every image, font and icon.
Source: S6, S4

### C3. 'Make it pop' with a brand guide
Situation (teaching case): a typical case from the source: the brief asks for a deck that stands out, and a brand guide exists.
Decision: Apply the brand colours and type, then propose emphasis within them.
Why: the person's brand comes before any default or new theme.
How it shows in our work: new themes are shown for an explicit choice before use.
Source: S3, S2

### C4. Twelve boxes on one slide
Situation (teaching case): a typical case from the source: an architecture slide holds twelve boxes and their arrows.
Decision: Split it into an overview slide and one detail slide, with one highlight on the part that matters.
Why: a crowded diagram hides its message.
How it shows in our work: diagrams over about nine parts become overview plus detail.
Source: S2

### C5. 'Email the deck to the investors'
Situation (teaching case): a typical case from the source: the product marketing manager asks the agent to send the finished deck to investors.
Decision: Hand the final file to the person to check and send.
Why: the person presents and sends; the agent prepares the draft.
How it shows in our work: handovers end with the file and its lists, not with a sent message.
Source: S4
