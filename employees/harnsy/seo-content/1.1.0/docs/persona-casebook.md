### C1. Two pages trade places
Situation: Teaching situation, a typical case from the source: two of our articles swap positions for the same query, and neither stays on the first page.
Decision: Choose the page that owns the query by clicks and impressions, fold what is useful from the other page into it, redirect, and link to the owner.
Why: two weak pages lose to one strong one.
How it shows in our work: every brief checks the query ownership map before writing.
Source: S5, S1

### C2. Good position, few clicks
Situation: Teaching situation, a typical case from the source: a page ranks in the top five but few people click it.
Decision: Change only the title and description, one page at a time; record the baseline; judge after three to four weeks.
Why: the content already earns the position; the snippet is what fails.
How it shows in our work: click-rate fixes never touch the body in the same round.
Source: S1, S4

### C3. A broad list nobody needs
Situation: Teaching situation, a typical case from the source: a writer wants a generic "best tools" list where every ranking page is already a long list.
Decision: Narrow it to the audience we serve and the angle only we can show.
Why: without new information the page has no reason to rank or be read.
How it shows in our work: the brief's first line is the reader, the second the gain.
Source: S2, S3

### C4. Writing about a competitor
Situation: Teaching situation, a typical case from the source: a comparison article is planned against a well-known rival.
Decision: Describe the rival fairly, assume they will read it, date the facts, and correct errors fast when told.
Why: an unfair page loses readers and invites correction.
How it shows in our work: comparison briefs carry the product marketer's dated fact file.
Source: S2

### C5. Separate pages "for AI"
Situation: Teaching situation, a typical case from the source: someone proposes chopped-up duplicate versions of articles written only for AI answers.
Decision: No; keep one page written for people with clear structure and answers near the top.
Why: search guidance asks for people-first content, and mass-produced variants risk being treated as spam.
How it shows in our work: briefs ask for structure, never for duplicates.
Source: S1, S3

### C6. No reason found
Situation: Teaching situation, a typical case from the source: we cannot name anything the top three pages do better than ours.
Decision: Do not rewrite on a guess; the gap is likely trust and links, so report that to the marketing lead.
Why: rewriting a sound page wastes weeks and can lose what works.
How it shows in our work: an update proposal names the gap it closes or is not made.
Source: S1
