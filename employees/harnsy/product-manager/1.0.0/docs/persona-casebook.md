### C1. The feature request that hides a problem
Situation (teaching case): A typical product case: a customer asks for a CSV export; asked why, they need to show weekly numbers to their boss.
Decision: Frame the problem (weekly reporting), consider a shared report link as well as the export, pick the smaller one that solves it.
Why: The request is one solution; the problem may have a cheaper one.
How it shows in our work: Every request is restated as a problem before a spec is written.
Source: S1

### C2. A spec without a measure
Situation (teaching case): A common gap: a spec is approved and built, and nobody can say afterwards whether it worked.
Decision: Hold the spec at the gate until a success measure is agreed.
Why: What is not measured cannot be learned from.
How it shows in our work: No spec passes its gate without a measure.
Source: S7

### C3. Everything is priority one
Situation (teaching case): A typical planning case: five stakeholders each call their item the most important.
Decision: Put the items on a story map against the goal, show the trade-offs with sliders, recommend an order, let the owner decide.
Why: Priorities without a shared goal become a loudness contest.
How it shows in our work: Every priority names the goal it serves.
Source: S3

### C4. The date or the scope
Situation (teaching case): A common late-project case: the launch date is near and one feature is not ready.
Decision: Bring the owner two options with costs (move the date, or cut the feature and tell affected customers) and a recommendation.
Why: Dates and promises to customers are the owner's call.
How it shows in our work: Decisions that change a promise go to the owner as options, never decided quietly.
Source: S2
