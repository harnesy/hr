# Position map

A position map puts competitors on two axes that buyers care about, so a team can see at a glance which areas are crowded and which are empty. It is honest only if every dot has a source and the axes come from buyers, not from what flatters your own product.

**Route:** `competitor-dossier` → **position-map** → `strategy-options` → the strategist decides.

## When to use

- After a dossier, before choosing where to compete.
- When a team says «nobody does X» and nobody has checked.
- Not as a public comparison graphic: public material goes through the copywriter and a lawyer.

## Inputs

- The dossier with its dated facts and agreed criteria.
- Your own product's facts, sourced the same way.

## Steps

1. **Choose two axes** from the buyers' criteria (for example price per seat, and depth of integrations). Each axis must be measurable from public facts. Write why these two matter to buyers.
2. **Define the scale** of each axis in plain terms (for example «integrations: 0 to 5 published connectors; 6 to 20; more than 20»), so another person would place the dots the same way.
3. **Place each rival** only where the dossier has a dated fact for both axes. Put the link and date next to each dot in the data table.
4. **List the rivals left off** and why (no public price, no documentation).
5. **Place your own product** by the same rules, from the same kind of sources.
6. **Read the map.** Name crowded areas and empty ones. For each empty area, list the possible reasons: room nobody has taken, an area that is hard to serve, or an area with no demand. Mark which reason has evidence.
7. **Segment if needed.** If buyers in different segments compare on different things, draw one map per segment.

## Rules

- **Axes from buyers,** never chosen to make your own product look good.
- **No dot without a source,** and every source dated.
- **An empty area is a question, not a gap to fill.**
- **Internal only.**

## Checks before hand-over

- Both axes come from the agreed criteria, with a written scale.
- Every dot has a link and a date in the data table.
- Rivals left off are listed with the reason.
- Each empty area lists possible reasons and the evidence for them.

## Artefacts

- `market/<topic>/position-map.md`: the axes and scales, the map (a simple drawing or table), the data table, the rivals left off, the reading of crowded and empty areas.

## Hand-over

- **To the marketing strategist:** the map and its reading.
- **To the product marketing manager:** the map, as input to positioning.
- **To `strategy-options`:** crowded and empty areas, with their evidence.
