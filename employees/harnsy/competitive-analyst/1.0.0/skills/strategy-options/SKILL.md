# Strategy options

A good strategy paper does not hand over one answer; it lays out two or three real choices with their costs and risks, including the choice to stay out, so the people who decide can see what they are trading. This skill writes those options from the evidence and recommends one; the owner decides.

**Route:** `competitor-dossier` + `position-map` → **strategy-options** → the marketing strategist or the founder decides → the plan.

## When to use

- A planning decision is coming: where to launch, which segment to serve, how to respond to a rival's move.
- A team is about to pursue the first idea without looking at another.
- Not for the marketing plan itself (the strategist's) or for public messaging (the product marketing manager's).

## Inputs

- The dossier and the position map, with dates.
- The decision, its owner and its date.
- Constraints: budget, team, time.

## Steps

1. **State the decision** as one question, with the owner and the date.
2. **Write two or three options that differ in kind,** for example: compete head-on in a segment; serve an area the map shows as empty; partner rather than compete; stay out of a segment. One option is always where not to compete.
3. **For each option, write:**
   - who it serves and what need it meets;
   - what it needs (people, money, time, capabilities);
   - the window: why now, and how long it may stay open;
   - barriers to entry and how much the segment matters to the rivals already there;
   - likely moves by rivals, stated as possibilities from evidence, never as certainties;
   - risks, and how each could be reduced;
   - cost, from a dated estimate where figures are used;
   - the signal that would change the recommendation (for example «if rival B publishes a lower plan»).
4. **Compare** the options side by side on the same points.
5. **Recommend one,** saying which piece of evidence tips it and how sure you are.
6. **Hand it to the owner** for the decision; record the decision and the date when it is made.

## Rules

- **Options, not orders.** The owner decides.
- **Evidence-based.** Every claim about the market links to the dossier or the map.
- **No forecasts as facts.** Revenue, share or rival moves are possibilities with their assumptions.
- **Fair to rivals.** No disparaging or unverifiable statements.

## Checks before hand-over

- Two or three options, differing in kind, one of them where not to compete.
- Each option covers the same points.
- Every market claim links to a dated source.
- The recommendation names the deciding evidence and the signal that would change it.

## Artefacts

- `market/<topic>/options.md`: decision, options, comparison table, recommendation, signals to watch, and the decision when made.

## Hand-over

- **To the marketing strategist or the founder:** the options paper for decision.
- **To the product marketing manager:** the chosen option, as input to positioning and messaging.
- **To the finance owner:** options with cost figures, to check the estimates.
