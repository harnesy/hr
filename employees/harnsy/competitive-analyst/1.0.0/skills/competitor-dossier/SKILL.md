# Competitor dossier

A dossier is a dated, sourced picture of the companies a customer could choose instead of you. It is only as good as its weakest source, so every fact carries a link and a date, and anything that is a rival's own marketing stays marked as their claim. Public sources only, always.

**Route:** a market question from the strategist or a founder → **competitor-dossier** → `position-map` → `strategy-options` → the strategist decides.

## When to use

- Before planning a launch, a price change, a new segment or a quarter's marketing.
- When a team argues about rivals from memory.
- When a dossier is older than its decision needs (prices and plans change often).

## Inputs

- The market or segment and the decision it informs, with its date.
- The rivals already known, and the criteria buyers compare on, if anyone knows them.

## Steps

1. **Agree the criteria first.** What do buyers compare on in this market (price, integrations, support, setup time…)? Write them down and agree them with the person who asked, before looking at any rival.
2. **List the rivals.** Direct competitors, related ones that serve part of the need, and substitutes from another industry (including «do it by hand» or «spreadsheet»). Cut to the four or five that matter most and say why.
3. **Start with existing sources:** company websites, pricing pages, documentation, public filings and annual reports, published case studies, job posts, public reviews, public statistics. Direct research (surveys, interviews with buyers) is done only by the owner's people, who say who they are.
4. **Per rival, record:** what they sell by product line and segment; published prices and plan limits; who they target; size or share signals; strengths and weaknesses against the agreed criteria; barriers they raise for a newcomer; how much your segment seems to matter to them.
5. **Per fact, record:** the claim, the link, the date read, the date on the page or «undated», and a confidence mark (high: primary source, dated; medium: primary, undated; low: secondary).
6. **Separate claims from facts.** «The rival says it is the top-rated product» is recorded as their claim, with its link, unless an independent source supports it.
7. **Mark gaps.** What could not be found publicly is written as a gap, never filled with a guess.
8. **Summarise** in a short table across rivals and criteria, each cell linked to its facts.

## Rules

- **Public sources only.** No pretexting, no fake accounts or false identities, no logins you were not given, no scraping against a site's terms, no paywalled material shared by others, no leaked documents, nothing from a rival's current or former staff. If such material is offered, decline it, do not read it, and note that it was declined.
- **Fair and checkable.** No disparaging, speculative or unverifiable statements; never say a rival lies, cheats or breaks the law.
- **Dated.** No fact without the date it was read.
- **Nothing published.** A dossier is internal; public comparison copy goes through the copywriter and a lawyer.

## Checks before hand-over

- Criteria were agreed before rivals were assessed.
- Four or five rivals, including at least one indirect competitor or substitute.
- Every fact has a link, a read date, a page date or «undated», and a confidence mark.
- Rivals' claims are labelled as claims.
- Gaps are listed; declined material is noted.

## Artefacts

- `market/<topic>/criteria.md`: agreed criteria and who agreed them.
- `market/<topic>/dossier-<rival>.md`: one per rival.
- `market/<topic>/summary.md`: the cross-rival table with links.

## Hand-over

- **To the marketing strategist:** the dossier and summary, as input to planning.
- **To `position-map` and `strategy-options`:** the facts and criteria.
- **To the copywriter, then a lawyer:** any fact meant for a public comparison, marked for a fresh check before publication.
