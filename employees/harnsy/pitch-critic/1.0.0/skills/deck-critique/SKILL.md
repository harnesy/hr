# Deck critique

A demanding reader goes through a deck asking one thing of every slide: what is claimed, and where is the evidence? This skill does the same before the real reader does. It follows the deck's own order, writes each claim down with the evidence found for it, and turns every gap into a question the founder can prepare for. It never says whether the company deserves money.

**Route:** a deck or business plan from the founder, the startup finance analyst or the presentation designer → **deck-critique** → the critique table to the founder → slide changes to the presentation designer, model changes to the startup finance analyst → unproven claims into the assumption log.

## When to use

- Before a deck or plan goes to outside readers (lenders, grant panels, early-stage investors, partners).
- Before rehearsing a meeting about the plan.
- Not for writing or designing the deck, not for building the financial model, and not for advice on whether anyone should put money into the company.

## Inputs

- The deck or plan, with its appendix and any data room index.
- Who will read it and at what stage the company is.
- The financial model, if the deck quotes it.

## Steps

1. **Read the deck once in its own order** before reading the founder's notes or anyone else's comments. Do not impose a template order.
2. **For each slide, list the claims,** one line each: market size, growth, customers, revenue, team experience, product stage, advantage over today's alternatives, costs, the amount asked.
3. **For each claim, record the evidence:**
   - **yes** — where it is (slide, appendix page, linked source with date);
   - **partly** — what is there and what is missing (a source without a date, a number without its basis);
   - **no** — nothing on the slide or in the appendix.
4. **Turn each «partly» or «no» into a question,** in plain words, specific enough that a number, a document or a source would answer it.
5. **Check the whole deck for what a reader expects to find:** what the company is, in one sentence; the problem and who has it; why the offer beats what people use today; the market and how customers are reached; how a sale happens; the team and how its experience fits the plan; the product and its stage; how much money is asked, for how long and for what; projections that match the request; the break-even point and when it is reached. List anything absent as «not covered».
6. **Cross-check the numbers between slides:** market size against customer count against revenue; spending against the amount asked; the funded months against the month of break-even. Every mismatch is a finding with both slide numbers.
7. **Rank the findings:** first those whose answer would change the plan, then those a reader will notice at once, then the rest.
8. **Compare** with the founder's notes and other comments after your own list is done.
9. **Log unproven claims** in the assumption log, with how each could be checked.

## Rules

- **Findings, not ratings.** The only verdict words are «evidence: yes / partly / no» and «question open / answered». No word rates the company, the price of its shares or the deal as a money decision, and no advice to put money in or keep it out.
- **Questions about the plan, never about the founders' private lives.**
- **No numbers are invented or improved.** Where a slide needs a number, the critique asks for it and its source.
- **Money and law go to professionals.** Projections, tax, ownership papers and securities rules go to the startup finance analyst, an accountant or a lawyer.
- **Respectful wording.** Pointed questions, polite sentences.

## Checks before hand-over

- Every slide has its claims, evidence status and questions.
- Every number mismatch names both slides.
- Every expected topic is either found (with its slide) or listed as «not covered».
- No sentence rates the company or advises on putting money in.

## Artefacts

- `pitch/<deck>/critique.md`: a table per slide (claim, evidence yes/partly/no with where, question), the «not covered» list, the number mismatches, the ranking.

## Hand-over

- **To the founder:** the critique, ranked.
- **To the presentation designer:** slide changes the founder accepts.
- **To the startup finance analyst:** number mismatches and questions about the model.
- **To the assumption log:** every unproven claim, with how to check it.
- **To a lawyer or an accountant (people):** legal, tax and ownership questions.
