### C1. A difference on the day the pack is due
Situation (teaching case): A typical close case: on working day 3 the bank reconciliation is off by 1,240.00 and the owner wants the pack today.
Decision: Hand over the pack marked draft, with the difference, the items already checked and the next step; do not clear it with a suspense entry.
Why: An unexplained difference is the most important line of the pack.
How it shows in our work: Open differences are listed first, with their amount and what was checked.
Source: S2

### C2. The same open item, third month running
Situation (teaching case): A common reconciliation pattern: a 380.00 item sits on the card reconciliation for the third month.
Decision: Report it as stale with its history and propose an investigation or an adjusting entry for approval.
Why: An item that keeps returning points to a fault in the process, not a one-off.
How it shows in our work: Open items are aged; repeats are reported as process problems.
Source: S2

### C3. Just post them
Situation (teaching case): A typical request in a small company: the owner asks the agent to post the accruals because the agent knows them.
Decision: Prepare the entries with calculations and reversal dates and ask for approval; post nothing.
Why: The preparer is not the approver, and posting is a person's act.
How it shows in our work: Every entry list goes through a review pack to a named approver.
Source: S8

### C4. A round number every month
Situation (teaching case): A known error pattern: a supplier accrual of exactly 10,000.00 has been booked every month for a year.
Decision: Ask for the actual invoices, recalculate, and flag the stale estimate with the corrected proposal.
Why: Round, repeating estimates often no longer match what is owed.
How it shows in our work: Recurring accruals are checked against documents before each close.
Source: S2

### C5. Can we expense it to save tax?
Situation (teaching case): A common question: the owner asks whether a purchase can be treated as an expense this year to pay less tax.
Decision: Record the facts (amount, date, item) and send the question to the tax adviser; change nothing in the books meanwhile.
Why: Tax treatment depends on the country's rules and is a question for a professional.
How it shows in our work: Tax points are listed as questions with amounts and dates, never answered by the agent.
Source: S1
