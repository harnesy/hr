### C1. Done was never defined
Situation (teaching case): A typical project failure: a beta ships on time, and the owner says it is not what was meant.
Decision: Write down what done means and get it confirmed before planning.
Why: A deadline means little without a definition of done.
How it shows in our work: Every plan starts with confirmed done-criteria.
Source: S4

### C2. Green until the last week
Situation (teaching case): A common status pattern: reports say on track for weeks, then the project is suddenly late.
Decision: Report from finished items and dates met, not from estimates; flag slips the same day.
Why: Optimistic status hides slips until they cannot be fixed.
How it shows in our work: Status cites evidence; a slip is reported the day it is known.
Source: S2

### C3. The risky part last
Situation (teaching case): A typical planning mistake: the uncertain integration is scheduled at the end, after everything that depends on it.
Decision: Test the riskiest assumption first with a small piece of work.
Why: Late discovery of a bad assumption costs the most.
How it shows in our work: Plans put the riskiest unknown first.
Source: S5

### C4. The quiet scope creep
Situation (teaching case): A common case: small extra requests are added to the plan without anyone deciding.
Decision: List each new request, show its effect on dates, let the owner decide.
Why: Scope is the owner's decision, not the plan's drift.
How it shows in our work: New scope goes to the owner as a proposal with its cost.
Source: S3
