# Negotiation prep

Most negotiations are decided before they start, by whoever prepared better. Preparation here means knowing what you want, what you will do if there is no agreement, what the thing is actually worth, and what the other side probably needs. This skill writes all of that down, with sources, so the user walks in with a floor, a plan and no surprises.

**Route:** one negotiation → **negotiation-prep** → `concession-plan` → `objection-replies` → the user negotiates.

## When to use

- Before any negotiation: a supplier contract, a renewal, a partnership, a price or scope change, a lease, a job offer.
- When an offer arrives and the user wants to know how to respond.
- Not for taking part in the talk, contacting the other side, or giving legal or tax advice.

## Inputs

- What is being negotiated, the documents, the deadline.
- The user's goals and limits.
- Public information about the other side.
- Who on the user's side may agree to what.

## Steps

1. **Rank the goals:** must have, want, nice to have, each in one line.
2. **Write the alternative if there is no deal:** the real next-best option, with evidence (a written quote, a known cost, a date), and its total cost including switching.
3. **Derive the walk-away point** from the alternative, and have the user confirm it in writing.
4. **Build an own estimate** of what the thing should cost or is worth, from your own analysis, with sources, dates and assumptions, before reading the other side's offer.
5. **Compare the offer** with the estimate once it is read; note what the offer leaves out or adds.
6. **Set out the other side's likely position:** goals, pressures, deadlines, their own alternatives, from public information only, each marked as a guess.
7. **List unknowns** to find out early, and how (a question in an early conversation, a document to request).
8. **Mark what the user will not disclose.**
9. **Scale the depth** to what is at stake, and say how deep the preparation went.
10. **List points for a professional:** legal clauses for a lawyer or the contract reviewer, tax effects for a tax adviser, employment terms for the user's own adviser.

## Rules

- **Sources and dates on every number,** or an «estimate» mark.
- **Public information only** about the other side; nothing private or improperly obtained.
- **The walk-away point is the user's,** confirmed by the user.
- **No legal or tax conclusions.** Those points are named and handed on.
- **Nothing sent by the agent.**

## Checks before hand-over

- Goals are ranked.
- The alternative has evidence; the walk-away point is confirmed by the user.
- The own estimate was written before the offer was read, with sources.
- Guesses about the other side are marked.
- Points for professionals are listed.

## Artefacts

- `negotiation/<deal>/prep.md`: goals, alternative and evidence, walk-away point, own estimate, offer comparison, the other side's likely position, unknowns, not to disclose, points for professionals.

## Hand-over

- **To `concession-plan`:** goals, walk-away point, tradeable items.
- **To the user:** the file.
- **To the contract reviewer or a lawyer:** contract wording; **to a tax adviser:** tax effects.
