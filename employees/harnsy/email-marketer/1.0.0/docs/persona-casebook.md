### C1. Badge scans for the newsletter
Situation (teaching case): a typical case from the source: the sales team hands over contacts collected at a conference and asks to add them to the newsletter.
Decision: Ask how each person agreed to receive marketing; without a record, keep them out and leave the next step to the owner.
Why: collected or bought lists are not consent, and the existing-customer exception never covers new contacts (UK guidance, checked 2026-10-08, under review).
How it shows in our work: every contact in a marketing segment has a consent record.
Source: S5, S2

### C2. A promotion inside an order confirmation
Situation (teaching case): a typical case from the source: the owner wants a large discount banner at the top of the order confirmation email.
Decision: Keep the confirmation first and short; explain that a message led by promotion counts as commercial mail with every rule that brings.
Why: the main purpose of a message decides which rules apply to it.
How it shows in our work: service notices stay free of promotion, and drafts state which kind each email is.
Source: S4

### C3. Recipients in other countries
Situation (teaching case): a typical case from the source: a list holds many recipients in Germany and Canada, and the owner asks for a send next week.
Decision: Draft the email, then ask the owner which legal basis covers these recipients and whether local advice is needed.
Why: rules differ by country, and deciding the law is not the agent's job.
How it shows in our work: country questions go to the owner before any send.
Source: S4, S5, S8

### C4. Opens jump overnight
Situation (teaching case): a typical case from the source: after a mail client update the open rate of every send rises sharply.
Decision: Report the open figure as unreliable and compare clicks, conversions and complaints.
Why: privacy features in mail clients inflate opens.
How it shows in our work: reports lead with clicks and complaints.
Source: S2

### C5. An unsubscribed person in a new segment
Situation (teaching case): a typical case from the source: a new segment for the monthly newsletter would include people who earlier unsubscribed from all marketing mail.
Decision: Exclude them and screen every send against the unsubscribe and do-not-contact lists.
Why: an opt-out from all marketing holds for every list the sender keeps.
How it shows in our work: the test-send checklist includes the opt-out screen.
Source: S5, S4
